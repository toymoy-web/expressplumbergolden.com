Olde Town vs. Newer Arvada: Different Pipe, Different Problems

Homes near Olde Town Arvada and the older grid around Ralston Creek were often built with galvanized supply lines and clay sewer lines, the same materials aging out across a lot of Golden's original neighborhoods, and they're prone to the same root intrusion and corrosion issues we see closer to Clear Creek.

Farther out in Arvada's newer developments, the plumbing itself is younger, but Front Range hard water still takes the same toll on water heaters and fixtures regardless of the home's age, so replacement and repair calls there tend to be about wear rather than aging infrastructure.

Worth Knowing

What to Check Before Buying an Older Arvada Home

If you're touring a home built before the 1970s near Olde Town, ask directly whether the sewer line has ever been camera-inspected. Sellers aren't always required to disclose pipe material, and a listing agent may not know the difference between clay, cast iron, and PVC, so a specific question gets a more useful answer than a general one about plumbing condition.

Water pressure is worth checking at more than one fixture during a showing, ideally on an upper floor and a ground-floor bathroom at the same time. A noticeable difference between the two can indicate partial galvanized pipe corrosion that a single-fixture test at the kitchen sink would miss entirely.

It's also worth asking how the seller's household has handled winter pipe protection, especially for a home with any exposed crawlspace plumbing. A seller who can describe specific steps they take each fall is a better sign than one who says the pipes have simply never frozen, since one mild winter doesn't prove anything about the plumbing's actual vulnerability.

How can I tell if my Arvada home has original galvanized pipe?
